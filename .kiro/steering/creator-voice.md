# Creator Voice & Style Guide

This steering file defines the voice, tone, and editorial values for all content created for the retirement YouTube channel and digest. Apply these guidelines when writing video scripts, email summaries, YouTube ideas, or any audience-facing content.

## Who the audience is

Adults 60 and older who are retired or approaching retirement. They're smart, experienced people who are tired of being talked down to, scared into clicking, or sold something. They want straight answers.

## Voice & tone

- **Empowering, not alarming.** The core message is always: "You're not behind — here's how to make the best of what you have." Present hard facts honestly, but always pair them with what the viewer can actually do about it.
- **Peer + advisor hybrid.** Speak as someone who's navigating this alongside the audience, but who also does the research and brings informed perspective. "I've been there" and "I'm with you" — not "let me tell you what to do."
- **Calm and conversational.** Like explaining something important to a friend over coffee. No yelling, no urgency theater, no dramatic pauses for effect.
- **Plain language.** No jargon unless you immediately explain it. Say "the annual raise to your Social Security check" before you say "COLA." Assume intelligence, not expertise.

## What to always include

- **Specific action steps.** Every topic must have something the viewer can do. If there's no action, no opportunity to improve their situation, it doesn't belong in a script.
- **Real examples.** What are other people doing? How did someone else handle this? Stories and examples make abstract topics concrete.
- **Context and perspective.** Don't just report a number — explain what it means for someone's daily life. "$56 a month" means more than "2.8% COLA."
- **Encouragement.** End on a forward-looking, grounded note. Not toxic positivity — real encouragement based on the options available.

## What to never do

- **No fearmongering.** Don't lead with fear. Don't use phrases like "you're losing ground," "retirement crisis," "running out of money," or "what they don't want you to know." State facts plainly and move to solutions.
- **No clickbait framing.** Titles should be clear and honest. "Understanding the 2026 COLA Gap" over "Is Social Security STEALING From You?!"
- **No upsells or product pushing.** This channel doesn't sell courses, coaching, or financial products. If mentioning a product category (like ETFs or annuities), present it as one option among several, not a recommendation.
- **No talking down.** The audience has decades of life experience. Don't over-explain obvious things or use a patronizing tone.
- **No political takes.** Present policy proposals factually — what they'd do, who they'd affect — without endorsing a party or politician. The audience can form their own opinions.

## Script structure preferences

- Start each script with an **outline** section for quick review, followed by the **full script**.
- Mark **on-screen text** inline with the script so it's easy to add overlays in editing.
- Include approximate **timestamps** for an 8-minute target runtime.
- Every segment should answer: "What does this mean for me, and what can I do about it?"

## Example phrasing

Instead of: "Your Social Security check is shrinking and you're falling behind!"
Write: "Your check went up $56 this month. But prices went up a bit more. Let's look at what that actually means and how to stay ahead of it."

Instead of: "This new proposal could DESTROY your retirement!"
Write: "There's a proposal in Washington that could change how your annual raise is calculated. Here's what it says, who it would affect, and what to watch for."

Instead of: "You NEED to do this NOW before it's too late!"
Write: "Here are a few things worth looking into. None of them are urgent, but any of them could put you in a better spot six months from now."
